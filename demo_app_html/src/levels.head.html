What Does the Model Get to See?

Five predictors learn the same half-life target from different descriptions of the peptide–HLA pair. The comparison asks whether sequence alone is enough, or whether a pretrained structural representation helps on unfamiliar examples.

Fair comparison

The rows, targets, and held-out splits stay fixed. What changes is the input representation and, for SE+XGB, the final regressor.

Results by holdout

Reading the splits

A · random is the easiest: rows are shuffled. B · peptide asks the model to handle unseen peptides. C · allele holds out whole alleles. C2e · cluster is the strictest, holding out groups of similar HLA grooves. All five models are evaluated on the same 9,031 rows across 54 alleles.

The main pattern is simple: the sequence models are competitive on shuffled rows, then lose correlation as the holdout becomes less familiar. The frozen Boltz-2 representation degrades less sharply. Use the tabs above to check that story against Pearson, rank correlation, and error in hours.

Two caveats

C2e has only 2 test clusters. Treat that column as a strong signal worth following up, not as a precise estimate of generalisation to every unseen groove.

SE changes feature weighting, not the raw input. It starts from the direct model’s same 704 channels, learns a gate over them, then feeds either an MLP or XGBoost. It is best read as a baseline variant rather than a separate rung in a hierarchy.
